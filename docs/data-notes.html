Appendix A — Data notes

This appendix records every data source and vintage used in the update, defines the geographies, and explains the reliability and comparison rules applied throughout the report.

A.2 Geography definitions

The study area is the PlanRVA region, held constant across every source through vectors defined once in the project’s shared setup file.

  • Region (8 localities): Charles City, Chesterfield, Goochland, Hanover, Henrico, New Kent, and Powhatan counties, plus Richmond city. This is the full regional analysis universe.
  • Primary localities (4): Chesterfield, Hanover, Henrico, and Richmond city carry the core regional narrative.
  • Secondary localities (4): Charles City, Goochland, New Kent, and Powhatan appear in local summaries only, always with reliability flags (see below).
  • Town of Ashland: a Census place (not a county) inside Hanover County, so it is pulled at the place level (summary level 160) in ACS and CHAS scripts and treated as a ninth local-summary geography.
  • Virginia serves as the statewide benchmark.

PUMS geography

Microdata estimates use 2020-vintage PUMAs, which do not follow county lines everywhere.

  • Core-3 (the regional PUMS default): Chesterfield (3 PUMAs), Henrico (2), and Richmond city (2) — the three localities that tile cleanly into whole PUMAs.
  • Hanover cannot be isolated in PUMS. It is split across two multi-county PUMAs — one covering eastern Hanover with King William, New Kent, and Charles City; the other covering western Hanover with Goochland and Powhatan — so no PUMA combination yields Hanover alone. Hanover enters the analysis only through county-level ACS tables.
  • A full-region PUMA set (core-3 plus the two mixed outer PUMAs) exists for region-wide totals where the extra counties are acceptable; it is used sparingly and labeled when it appears.

A.3 Estimate reliability

ACS estimates for small geographies carry sampling error, flagged consistently from each estimate’s coefficient of variation (CV, on a 0–100 scale).

  • High reliability: CV at or below 15 — reported without qualification.
  • Medium reliability: CV above 15 and at or below 30 — reported with a footnote identifying the cell as medium-reliability.
  • Low reliability: CV above 30 — the cell is suppressed rather than reported.
  • Reliability treatment applies to every secondary-locality and Ashland ACS estimate in the report.
  • Small PIT and eviction counts are reported as counts with a volatility caveat, never converted to rates.

A.4 Comparing to the 2022 Framework

Every section compares its findings to the 2022 Richmond Regional Housing Framework, but the two cycles rest on different survey windows.

  • This update’s anchor is the 2020–2024 ACS 5-year; the 2022 Framework used the 2016–2020 ACS 5-year. Overlapping multi-year windows differ by construction, so some movement between the two reports is structural rather than real-world change.
  • Changes since 2022 are therefore logged and narrated, never gated: a large swing prompts human review but never fails a build.
  • The 2022 baseline figures are transcribed from the published 2022 report, not recomputed, so the comparison is always against the numbers PHA actually published.
  • The 2022 report defined “the region” as the four primary localities only; regional comparisons against the 2022 baseline carry that narrower scope rather than the full 8-locality region.
