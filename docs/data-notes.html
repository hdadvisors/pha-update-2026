Appendix A — Data notes

This appendix records every data source and vintage used in the update, defines the geographies, and explains the reliability and comparison rules applied throughout the report.

A.2 Geography definitions

The study area is the PlanRVA region, held constant across every source through vectors defined once in the project’s shared setup file.

  • Region (8 localities): Charles City, Chesterfield, Goochland, Hanover, Henrico, New Kent, and Powhatan counties, plus Richmond city. This is the full regional analysis universe.
  • Primary localities (4): Chesterfield, Hanover, Henrico, and Richmond city carry the core regional narrative.
  • Secondary localities (4): Charles City, Goochland, New Kent, and Powhatan appear in local summaries only, always with reliability flags (see below).
  • Town of Ashland: a Census place (not a county) inside Hanover County, so it is pulled at the place level (summary level 160) in ACS and CHAS scripts and treated as a ninth local-summary geography.
  • Virginia serves as the statewide benchmark.

PUMS geography

Microdata estimates use 2020-vintage PUMAs, which do not follow county lines everywhere.

  • Core-3 (the regional PUMS default): Chesterfield (3 PUMAs), Henrico (2), and Richmond city (2) — the three localities that tile cleanly into whole PUMAs.
  • Hanover cannot be isolated in PUMS. It is split across two multi-county PUMAs — one covering eastern Hanover with King William, New Kent, and Charles City; the other covering western Hanover with Goochland and Powhatan — so no PUMA combination yields Hanover alone. Hanover enters the analysis only through county-level ACS tables.
  • A full-region PUMA set (core-3 plus the two mixed outer PUMAs) exists for region-wide totals where the extra counties are acceptable; it is used sparingly and labeled when it appears.

A.3 Estimate reliability

ACS estimates for small geographies carry sampling error, flagged consistently from each estimate’s coefficient of variation (CV, on a 0–100 scale).

  • High reliability: CV at or below 15 — reported without qualification.
  • Medium reliability: CV above 15 and at or below 30 — reported with a footnote identifying the cell as medium-reliability.
  • Low reliability: CV above 30 — the cell is suppressed rather than reported.
  • Reliability treatment applies to every secondary-locality and Ashland ACS estimate in the report.
  • Small PIT and eviction counts are reported as counts with a volatility caveat, never converted to rates.

Race and ethnicity crosswalk

Homeownership by race and ethnicity comes from the nine letter-suffix versions of ACS Table B25003, each carrying the same total, owner-occupied, and renter-occupied structure as B25003 itself. All nine are pulled and exported at the published grain; the report collapses them to six groups.

Report group Source tables 2024 households (region)
White, non-Hispanic B25003H 246,502
Black B25003B 117,413
Hispanic or Latino B25003I 27,491
Multiracial B25003G 24,300
Asian B25003D 17,305
Another race B25003C + B25003E + B25003F 14,430
  • Another race combines three published categories — American Indian and Alaska Native alone, Native Hawaiian and Other Pacific Islander alone, and some other race alone. Each is too small to report on its own: Native Hawaiian and Other Pacific Islander alone is under 200 households region-wide, with a CV above 40 at every grain and in every year. Combined counts add and their margins of error combine in quadrature.
  • B25003A (White alone) is not used. The report’s White group is B25003H, which excludes Hispanic or Latino householders. B25003A remains in the row-level export for anyone who needs it.
  • The six groups overlap, and do not sum to the household total. Only the White group excludes Hispanic or Latino householders, so a Hispanic Black household is counted in both Black and Hispanic or Latino. B25003 publishes no race-alone-not-Hispanic tables, so the overlap cannot be removed at this table’s grain. Rates by group are still valid; a sum across groups is not.
  • Regional figures pool the four primary localities, matching every other regional aggregate in this update. The nine published categories are also carried for each of the eight region localities and Virginia. There is no Ashland: at the town’s size nearly every group falls below the CV-30 threshold.
  • Years are 2010-2014, 2015-2019, and 2020-2024 — the same non-overlapping windows used for overall tenure, so a rate by group can be read against the overall rate for the same period.
  • Reliability is computed per geography, group, and year. Because an ownership rate rests on the owner and the renter count together, the export also carries a rate-level tier taking the worse of the two; the figures suppress on that, not on the single-cell tier.

A.4 Ownership and rental affordability

The housing-gap chapter’s income-needed and maximum-affordable-price figures use a standard rule set, applied consistently through r/affordcalc.R.

  • Rental affordability: a household can afford a rent at or below 30 percent of its gross monthly income. Income needed for a given rent is rent × 12 / 0.30.
  • Ownership affordability: a household can afford a home priced so that the monthly principal, interest, tax, and insurance (PITI) payment is at or below 28 percent of its gross monthly income.
  • Ownership assumptions are provisional placeholders, pending PHA input: a 10 percent down payment, a 30-year fixed term at the current Freddie Mac PMMS rate, property tax at 1.0 percent of home value annually, and $1,500/year in homeowners insurance — one regional figure rather than a per-locality rate. Private mortgage insurance is not modeled. PHA review of these figures against local lending norms is expected to refine them.
  • AMI bands come from HUD’s FY2026 Section 8 Income Limits for the region’s single HUD area (“Richmond, VA HUD Metro FMR Area,” confirmed to cover all 8 rr counties identically rather than assumed). HUD publishes 30, 50, and 80 percent limits by household size; 100 and 120 percent are derived by applying the same household-size scaling factor embedded in the 50 percent limits to the published median family income (r/hud_ami.R). The gaps chapter’s price-point figures reference the 4-person limit as the headline “area median income,” the same convention HUD and NLIHC use for headline AMI reporting.
  • The starter-home cut in gaps.qmd compares a current-dollar (FY2026) income threshold against CHAS 2018-2022 renter households grouped by relative-AMI band — a vintage mismatch (current income limits vs. a 2018-2022 area median) stated as a caveat in the chapter, not resolved, pending the PUMS-based rebuild.

A.5 Comparing to the 2022 Framework

Every section compares its findings to the 2022 Richmond Regional Housing Framework, but the two cycles rest on different survey windows.

  • This update’s anchor is the 2020–2024 ACS 5-year; the 2022 Framework used the 2016–2020 ACS 5-year. Overlapping multi-year windows differ by construction, so some movement between the two reports is structural rather than real-world change.
  • Changes since 2022 are therefore logged and narrated, never gated: a large swing prompts human review but never fails a build.
  • The 2022 baseline figures are transcribed from the published 2022 report, not recomputed, so the comparison is always against the numbers PHA actually published.
  • The 2022 report defined “the region” as the four primary localities only; regional comparisons against the 2022 baseline carry that narrower scope rather than the full 8-locality region.
